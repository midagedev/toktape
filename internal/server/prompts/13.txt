Our payments service on Kubernetes restarts roughly every forty minutes under normal load. It is a Spring Boot application on Java 21, and nothing in its own logs mentions an error before the restart.

Explain what is killing the container and why the JVM never throws OutOfMemoryError first. Show how the heap percentage, metaspace, thread stacks, code cache and direct buffers add up against the 1 GiB limit, and what the Hikari warning and the GC pause each tell you. Then give a plan to confirm it on a live pod and the changes you would make.

The captures below are the material: the pod as Kubernetes sees it, the manifest that set its limits, one complete cycle of the application log, the node's record of the kill, what the container's own cgroup and process status said seventeen seconds before it died, a staging run of the same revision, on the JDK image, with NativeMemoryTracking on, and the application's configuration.

The workload around it.

Each replica serves about 40 requests a second of card authorisation posts, with a p99 latency SLO of 300 milliseconds that a healthy pod meets at 165 to 190. Thirty-one of the forty are the authorisation posts themselves; the rest are status polls from the merchant portal. The rate does not drop at night: settlement runs around the clock, so Sunday 03:00 is an ordinary load and the cycle above is the "under normal load" of the opening paragraph, not a quiet-hours anomaly. The settlement exporter is the piece that changed this release; from the 2.14.3 release notes: "settlement: the export now runs every 30 minutes instead of nightly at 02:40, and rows are streamed to object storage as they are produced rather than written at the end (settlement.export.buffered=true)."

For what the same export looked like before the change, the last night of 2.14.2, from the same pod's log, Thursday into Friday:

~~~
02:40:00 INFO  Settlement export started: 528417 rows pending from the 02:00 checkpoint
02:41:19 WARN  GC pause (G1 Evacuation Pause) 180ms, heap 388M->302M
02:43:52 WARN  GC pause (G1 Evacuation Pause) 240ms, heap 501M->411M
02:44:38 INFO  Exported 528417 settlement rows to object storage (buffered=false)
~~~

Four minutes thirty-eight seconds for twenty-nine times the rows, and the pod held its heap. The export runs on every pod independently — each pod exports the settlement rows pending since its last checkpoint, its own traffic plus whatever earlier lives of this pod produced and never got out: a life killed mid-export never advances the checkpoint, so its whole pending set carries to the next life, and the backlog a kill leaves behind is itself part of the damage — which is why the whole fleet cycles and not one unlucky lock-holder. The 18204 rows pending at the 03:30 export are about three and a half per cent of the 528,417 the old nightly export moved — fifty minutes of rows at the old rate, set against the sixteen minutes of traffic this life itself had produced — which is the shape of an accumulated backlog, not of one life's own output. No payments pod was ever OOMKilled on 2.14.2; the first OOMKilled restart after the rollout came 42 minutes after it, Friday 10:22.

The first hypothesis in the incident channel was "a memory leak in the new exporter", stated and untested. The captures above are everything the team has gathered so far, and nothing about the deployment has been changed since Friday's rollout. What a live pod offers whoever watches the next one: kubectl exec and port-forward work while it lives, the image is a JRE (no jcmd, no jstack in production; staging runs the JDK image), bash and curl are present, /proc and /sys/fs/cgroup are readable from inside, actuator is up on /actuator/health and /actuator/prometheus, the nodes run cgroup v2, and the forty-minute cycle is reliable enough to watch one happen from the first minute.
